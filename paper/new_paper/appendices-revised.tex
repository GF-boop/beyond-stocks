\section{Properties of the reconstructed managed-futures proxy}
\label{app:mf-proxy}
Managed futures enter through a reconstructed rule, so the construction and
its external comparisons deserve separate scrutiny. The rule combines
momentum signals at several horizons and scales positions using lagged
volatility. The economic motivation follows \citet{MoskowitzOoiPedersen2012}
and \citet{HurstOoiPedersen2017}. Agreement with a commercial index is a
descriptive check; it cannot establish an investable historical track record.

\subsection{Monthly inputs and the annual simulation series}
\label{app:mf-monthly-data}
The annual lifecycle panel and the monthly signal inputs are distinct data
layers. The monthly snapshot supplies the managed-futures construction;
complete calendar years are then compounded for the annual simulation.
The source table records the original reconstruction's inputs and
transformations. Price-only equities, synthetic bond returns, omitted
commodity carry and roll, and incomplete early currency coverage limit the
economic interpretation of the reconstructed return.

The annual simulation does not resolve intrayear trading, drawdowns, or
margin requirements. Its revised currency treatment applies a forward to
the initial foreign principal. Exchange-rate exposure on the subsequent
asset gain or loss remains. This differs from removing foreign collateral
and replacing it with the resident bill for the entire terminal asset value.

\section{Historical-sample uncertainty}
\label{app:historical-uncertainty}
Monte Carlo intervals measure sampling noise conditional on the observed
history and the specified model. They do not measure uncertainty about the
historical return-generating process. We retain 1927--2025 as the main
sample. The revised 1950--2025 and 1970--2025 experiments, each with
10,000 paths, address sensitivity to the historical window. They are
reported in the main text and stored under
\path{results/method_review/sample_windows/}. These overlapping periods
are selected historical comparisons, not independent validation samples.

The revised source-country omissions remove the country from resident
rows, international-equity constituents, and world-bond constituents.
At 5,000 paths per omission, ACO ruin ranges from 7.10\% to 8.66\%;
the proportional 200\% family ranges from 1.06\% to 3.18\%, and the
equal-weight 200\% family from 1.72\% to 3.54\%.
The corresponding saving rates range from 5.00\% to 5.26\% and from
5.63\% to 6.16\%, against each sample's 10\% reference.
These are ranges across omissions, not confidence intervals.
The source audit is \path{paper/figures/source_country_sensitivity.json}.

The separate event experiments remove Italy 1942, then Italy 1942 and
France 1946; the country experiment removes Italy throughout.
Their reconstructed inputs and outputs are under
\path{results/method_review/source_exclusions/}.
They measure the influence of observations identified during the audit.
They do not establish that those observations are errors, and their
exclusions do not reconstruct every underlying monthly managed-futures
input. The main text reports these results.

\section{Reproducibility and Conversion Checks}
\label{app:reproducibility}
The revised baseline is identified by 1,561 resident country-years,
10,000 paths, seed 20260827, ten-year mean blocks, and the
\texttt{fixed\_notional} hedge convention. Source files, configurations,
and outputs must be checked together: sharing a seed does not make paths
identical across different historical panels.

The annual conversion and simulation can be reproduced from the supplied
annual inputs. The monthly managed-futures reconstruction has an upstream
engine dependency outside this paper directory. Accordingly, the package's
ability to consume the frozen monthly output must be distinguished from
an autonomous reconstruction of that output from the original providers.
The external-index comparisons also depend on their licensed
inputs. Neither limitation is removed by a successful LaTeX compilation.

For a foreign asset's local real return $R^L_f$ and inflation $\pi_f$,
the nominal gross return is $(1+R^L_f)(1+\pi_f)$. A fixed-notional
forward covers the initial foreign principal. If $q$ is the realized
resident-currency value of one unit of foreign currency relative to its
initial value, $G_f$ the foreign nominal gross asset return, and
$B_c/B_f$ the covered-interest-parity forward factor, the hedged nominal
gross payoff is
\[
 G^{H}_{f\to c}=\frac{B_c}{B_f}+(G_f-1)q.
\]
Deflating by resident inflation produces the real return used in the
revised panel. The second term retains currency exposure on the foreign
gain or loss. Consequently, equality of all residents' asset excess
returns is not a valid check for this convention. That identity belongs
to the earlier idealized full-terminal-value hedge.

The return-cap diagnostic, ideal hedge, legacy block restart, restricted
historical windows, and source exclusions are distinct experiments.
Their output files identify the changed convention.
The revised model does not silently discard a return because it is large.
Documented market-year exclusions and influence diagnostics have different
interpretations and should remain separately identifiable.

\section{Margin calls on the do-it-yourself route}
\label{app:margin-call}
The earlier margin exercise modeled annual rebalancing and a 25\%
maintenance threshold on a directly financed account. Its reported absence
of year-end calls was a result of that earlier annual sample and
convention. It is not a revised estimate of margin-call risk.
The exercise cannot observe a breach and liquidation within the year.

For initial equity normalized to one, gross assets $L$, debt $L-1$,
asset-book nominal gross return $G_A$, and debt gross accrual $G_D$,
the year-end equity fraction is
\[
 m_{\mathrm{end}}=
 \frac{L G_A-(L-1)G_D}{L G_A}.
\]
A maintenance call at threshold $m$ occurs when
$G_A < (L-1)G_D/[L(1-m)]$, provided the asset value remains positive.
The threshold therefore depends on financing as well as exposure.
Nominal rather than real account values determine the margin test.

An annual check gives no bound on intrayear calls, variation margin,
liquidity needs, changing broker requirements, or losses caused by forced
deleveraging. Holding an unlevered position in a fund may move margin
management inside the fund, but it does not remove the fund's financing,
liquidity, or deleveraging risk. The lifecycle results are consequently
conditional portfolio-return experiments, not an operational validation
of a particular fund or brokerage implementation.
