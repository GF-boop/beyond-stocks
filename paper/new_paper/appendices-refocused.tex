\section{Data appendix}
\label{app:data-reconstruction}

This appendix describes the construction of the asset class returns. Section~\ref{app:panel} describes
the country-year panel and the conversion of returns into the investor's currency.
Section~\ref{app:gold} describes gold returns. Section~\ref{app:mf-proxy} describes the construction of
the managed-futures series and compares it with commercial trend-following indexes and funds.

\subsection{Country-year panel and local real returns}
\label{app:panel}\label{app:returns}

The panel begins with release 6 of the Jord\`a-Schularick-Taylor Macrohistory Database
\citep{MacrohistoryDB,JordaEtAl2019}. We extend each series through 2025 using dividend-adjusted local
indexes, locally listed exchange-traded funds, and the Global Macro Database
\citep{MullerXuLehbibChen2025}. A source indicator identifies the appended years.

Let $S_{c,f,t}$ denote units of the investor's currency $c$ per unit of foreign currency $f$. If
$R^{N}_{f,t}$ is a nominal return in foreign currency and $\pi_{c,t}$ is local inflation, the local real
return on an unhedged foreign asset is
\begin{equation}
  1+R^{(c)}_{f,t}=(1+R^{N}_{f,t})\frac{S_{c,f,t}}{S_{c,f,t-1}}\frac{1}{1+\pi_{c,t}}.
  \label{eq:conversion}
\end{equation}
We apply this conversion to international stocks and gold. The international stock basket excludes the
investor's country and weights foreign markets by lagged market capitalization, constructed from the
market-capitalization-to-GDP ratios of \citet{KuvshinovZimmermann2022}, with real GDP weights from the
Maddison Project (via the Macrohistory Database and \citealt{MullerXuLehbibChen2025}) when these ratios
are unavailable.

Figure~\ref{fig:cumwealth} plots the cumulative real return on each asset class for a US investor. The
logarithmic scale makes proportional changes comparable across dates.

\subsection{Gold}
\label{app:gold}

Gold returns use the annual US dollar price from MeasuringWorth \citep{OfficerWilliamsonMeasuringWorth}
through 1967 and the London Bullion Market Association afternoon fixing \citep{LBMA} from 1968. Both are
quoted in dollars per troy ounce, and we splice them without adjustment. We deduct custody costs of 0.40\%
per year and convert the dollar return into local currency using equation~\eqref{eq:conversion}. Before
1968, the dollar price was administered, and exchange-rate parities under the gold standard and the
Bretton Woods system also constrained gold prices in other currencies. US residents regained the right to
hold gold at the end of 1974 \citep{USMintGold1974}.

\subsubsection{Monthly inputs}
\label{app:mf-monthly-data}

The managed-futures series is constructed from monthly data and then compounded into calendar years for
the annual simulations. Table~\ref{tab:mf-monthly-sources} describes the monthly inputs. The source
families are OECD and FRED national equity and yield series \citep{OECDMEI,FRED}, NBER historical series
\citep{NBERMacrohistory}, World Bank commodity prices \citep{WorldBankPinkSheet}, and Federal Reserve H.10
exchange rates \citep{FedH10}. Bond futures are approximated by synthetic excess returns on par ten-year
bonds, equity futures by price index returns, and currency forwards by lagged three-month interest
differentials. The first month of each source segment receives no return, so that differences in price
levels across sources do not enter the trading signal. The series earns the US dollar cash return on its
collateral once. For the annual simulations, its dollar net asset value is treated as a foreign asset and
hedged in the same manner as global bonds.

\section{Methods appendix}
\label{app:methods}

This appendix provides additional details on the portfolio returns, the bootstrap, the risk parity
weights, the properties of the household problem, and the experiments reported in the paper.

\subsection{Currency hedge and portfolio returns}
\label{app:hedge}

Let $A^f_t$ be the gross nominal return on a foreign asset, $B^f_t$ and $B^c_t$ the gross nominal bill
returns in the foreign and local currencies, and $q_t=S_{c,f,t}/S_{c,f,t-1}$ the change in the exchange
rate. The hedge sells the initial foreign principal forward at the covered-interest-parity rate, so the
local real return on the hedged position is
\begin{equation}
 1+R^{c,H}_t=\frac{B^c_t/B^f_t+(A^f_t-1)q_t}{1+\pi_{c,t}}.
 \label{eq:covered}
\end{equation}
Gains and losses beyond the initial principal remain exposed to the exchange rate. Realized annual bill
returns proxy for the interest rates that price the forward. The managed-futures series already includes
its US dollar collateral return, and the forward payoff is added according to
equation~\eqref{eq:covered}. The hedging cost of 0.10\% per year applies to the hedged notional.

For a levered portfolio, the coefficient on the local bill return in equation~\eqref{eq:return} of the
paper is negative. Holding asset returns fixed, a higher ex post real bill return lowers the portfolio
return, and an inflation surprise that lowers the real bill return raises it.

\subsection{Bootstrap continuation and local currencies}
\label{app:bootstrap}

When a bootstrap block reaches a gap or the end of a country's data, the block continues at the first
available year of another country, selected through a uniformly drawn panel row. Countries are therefore
weighted by the length of their samples. Each country-year contains returns measured in that country's
currency and price index, so a block from Japan in the 1980s is a consistent sequence of yen-denominated
real returns. A simulated lifetime can join real return histories from several countries. The simulation
does not model a currency exchange at these joins.

\subsection{Risk parity weights}
\label{app:allocation}\label{app:leverage-ladders}

For a positive definite covariance matrix $\widehat\Sigma$, the risk parity weights solve
\begin{equation}
 x^*=\arg\min_{x_i>0}\left\{\frac12 x'\widehat\Sigma x-\frac14\sum_{i=1}^4\log x_i\right\},\qquad
 v=x^*/(\mathbf1'x^*).
\end{equation}
The first-order conditions imply $x_i(\widehat\Sigma x)_i=1/4$, so each asset class contributes equally to
portfolio variance \citep{MaillardRoncalliTeiletche2010,RoncalliRiskBudgeting2014}. The covariance matrix
is the sample covariance of annual real returns on the four asset classes, net of fees, custody, and
hedging costs, across all 1,561 country-years with equal weights. We apply no shrinkage or winsorization.
All eigenvalues are positive, and the computed risk contributions differ from 25\% by less than $10^{-7}$.
The weights are 14.53\% stocks, 47.47\% global bonds, 12.05\% gold, and 25.95\% managed futures. Borrowing
costs are added only after the weights are scaled to the target exposure, so the equal contributions apply
to the unlevered portfolio.

For the comparison at equal volatility, we scale the weights of each diversified strategy by the gross
exposure $G$ at which the sample standard deviation of its annual real return, including financing and
hedging costs, equals the 25.28\% of the unlevered all-equity strategy across the 1,561 country-years. We
solve for $G$ by bisection. The resulting exposures are 166\% for the proportional strategy, 265\% for
risk parity, 126\% for stocks, gold, and managed futures, and 132\% for stocks and managed futures. When
we reduce managed-futures returns, we hold these exposures fixed.

\subsection{Properties of the household problem}
\label{app:framework}\label{sec:utility-framework}

This section derives the two properties of the household problem used in the paper. Let $v$ denote the
asset class proportions, $G$ gross exposure, and $r_c$ the savings rate, and let
$V(r_c,v,G)=E[U(C,B)]$ denote expected utility.

\paragraph{Invariance of ruin to the savings rate.}
For a fixed strategy and simulated path, wealth is proportional to the savings rate. Let $A(v,G)$ be
retirement wealth, $d_t(v,G)$ the withdrawal actually paid in retirement, and $b(v,G)$ the bequest, all at
a unit savings rate. Then
\begin{equation}
 D=0.04\,r_cA,\qquad C_t=\max\{SS_t+r_c d_t,SSI_t\},\qquad B=r_cb.
 \label{eq:scale-wealth}
\end{equation}
The planned and actual withdrawals scale together, so the event of exhausting wealth does not depend on
$r_c>0$. Table~\ref{tab:restored-policy} shows that ruin probabilities are identical across savings rates.
Fixing the withdrawal at 4\% of the all-equity strategy's retirement wealth breaks this proportionality,
which is why the path-matched income experiment measures a distinct outcome.

\paragraph{Equivalent savings rates and marginal utility of return.}
$V$ increases with $r_c$ whenever additional saving raises consumption above the floor or the bequest. At
a locally unique equivalent savings rate with $V_{r_c}>0$, the implicit function theorem gives, for an
allocation parameter $a$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial r_c^*}{\partial a}=-\frac{V_a}{V_{r_c}}.
 \label{eq:saving-gradient}
\end{equation}
Let $\widetilde R_{t}$ be the vector of net asset class returns and $M_t=\partial U/\partial R^p_t$ the
sensitivity of lifetime utility to the portfolio return in year $t$, which includes all subsequent effects
on wealth, withdrawals, consumption, and the bequest. For a change in proportions $h$ with $\mathbf1'h=0$,
and under conditions that allow differentiation inside the expectation \citep{Glasserman1991},
\begin{align}
 V_h&=G\sum_t\Bigl(E[M_t]\,E[h'\widetilde R_t]+\mathrm{Cov}(M_t,h'\widetilde R_t)\Bigr),
 \label{eq:composition-value}\\
 V_G&=E\!\left[\sum_t M_t\bigl(v'\widetilde R_t-R^f_t-\phi\bigr)\right],\qquad G>1.
 \label{eq:exposure-value}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:composition-value} separates the value of a change in weights into an average-return
term and a term that measures whether the added asset pays off when the marginal utility of return is
high. Equation~\eqref{eq:exposure-value} shows that leverage adds value only when the utility-weighted
portfolio return exceeds the bill return plus the borrowing spread. Differentiability fails on paths where
the household's wealth is exhausted or the consumption floor begins to bind, and such paths occur with
positive probability in a discrete bootstrap. The derivatives therefore describe small changes in weights,
and the paper reports exact changes. The risk parity conditions do not imply that either derivative is
zero, so the risk parity weights need not maximize household utility even with a known covariance matrix.

\subsection{Financing routes}
\label{app:funding-routes}\label{app:implementation}

The borrowing spread of 0.30\% is an annual spread above the local bill rate. Futures and box spreads on
European-style index options provide financing close to this level \citep{CMEBoxFinancing2024}.
\citet{WangBoxFinancing2025} reports implied box-spread financing rates of 0.30\% to 0.50\% above three- and
five-year Treasury yields in February 2025. Access, maturity, collateral requirements, and execution costs
differ across these routes. The model separately charges managed-futures fees and trading costs, gold
custody, and hedging costs, and it does not charge an additional cost for annual rebalancing. The
volatility-scaled positions inside the managed-futures series are distinct from the portfolio's gross
exposure, which measures the allocation to each asset class.

Holding leveraged fund shares differs from borrowing against pledged assets. Direct borrowing can force
liquidation or require additional collateral, whereas a leveraged fund can reduce exposure internally. The
annual model sets depleted wealth to zero and does not simulate forced liquidation within the year.

\subsection{Resampled calendar histories}
\label{app:historical-uncertainty}\label{app:history}

The analysis in Table~\ref{tab:restored-history} measures uncertainty about the historical record rather
than simulation error given that record. We resample blocks of calendar-year cross-sections from the
panel. Each cross-section retains all countries observed in that year and their joint returns. The outer
blocks follow the stationary block bootstrap of \citet{PolitisRomano1994} with circular wrapping and average
lengths of 5, 10, or 20 years. Each of 100 outer histories contains 99 calendar years, which are relabeled
consecutively before the household simulation. Each history supports 1,000 household simulations with the
ten-year average block length of the paper. The strategies are held at their base-case equal-volatility
exposures in every history.

\subsection{Historically investable asset classes}
\label{app:availability}

The historical availability analysis retains the 1927 to 2025 sample but sets the gold weight to zero
before 1975 and adds managed-futures sectors as their futures markets opened, starting from commodities
and adding currencies in 1972, interest rates in 1977, and equity indexes in 1982
\citep{CMEContractDates2026,USMintGold1974}. The removed weight is reallocated in proportion to the
remaining weights at unchanged gross exposure. For 1960 to 1963, the restricted managed-futures series
lacks twelve months of signals, and its weight is also reallocated. At 175\% exposure, the restricted
proportional strategy has a ruin probability of 2.26\% and an equivalent savings rate of 5.77\%.

\subsection{First-order decomposition}
\label{app:local-decomposition}

equations~\eqref{eq:composition-value} and~\eqref{eq:exposure-value} at the initial weights and compare

\subsection{Risk aversion}
\label{app:gamma-sensitivity}

Table~\ref{tab:restored-gamma} reports equivalent savings rates for the all-equity and proportional
strategies at 200\% exposure for risk aversion parameters from 2 to 10. We consider two conventions for
the bequest intensity. The joint calibration holds fixed the monthly intensity of
\citet{AnarkulovaCederburgODoherty2025}, so that the annual intensity changes with $\gamma$. The fixed
calibration holds the annual intensity at $\theta=2{,}360$. The proportional strategy requires less
saving than the unlevered all-equity strategy under every specification.

The equivalent savings rates of the levered all-equity strategy vary non-monotonically with $\gamma$ for
two reasons. First, at $\gamma=3$ the joint calibration implies a bequest intensity about eight times
larger than the fixed calibration, and the bequest accounts for 52\% of the negative expected utility of the
levered all-equity strategy. The frequent ruin of this strategy leaves no bequest, which explains its
equivalent savings rate of 20.09\% under the joint calibration, compared with 12.42\% under the fixed
calibration. Second, as $\gamma$ rises, expected utility is increasingly determined by the lowest
consumption paths. The worst 1\% of lifetimes account for 19\% of the negative expected utility of the
levered all-equity strategy at $\gamma=3.84$, 30\% at $\gamma=5$, 48\% at $\gamma=7.5$, and 61\% at
$\gamma=10$. The equivalent savings rate then reflects a small number of paths in which consumption falls
toward the Social Security and SSI floor, and it need not be monotonic in $\gamma$. For $\gamma\geq5$, the
bequest accounts for less than 0.02\% of expected utility, and the two conventions give the same
equivalent savings rates.

\subsection{Margin requirements}
\label{app:margin-call}

The 25\% threshold corresponds to the maintenance requirement for long margin-eligible equities in US
Regulation T accounts \citep{FINRAMargin2021}. The equity fraction of an account with gross assets $L$ and
debt $L-1$ at the start of the year is
\begin{equation}
 m_{\text{end}}=\frac{LG_A-(L-1)G_D}{LG_A}
\end{equation}
at year-end, where $G_A$ is the gross nominal asset return and $G_D$ is the gross nominal cost of debt,
including the borrowing spread. Table~\ref{tab:restored-margin} reports year-end breaches in the
bootstrap simulations. Table~\ref{tab:monthly-margin} uses monthly US dollar data from 1970 to 2025 to
compare month-end and year-end tests on the same paths.

\subsection{Personal taxes}
\label{app:taxation}

Our returns are net of implementation costs but before personal taxes. Gold, managed-futures
distributions, rebalancing gains, and interest expense can be taxed differently. For example, the tax
disclosure of the SPDR Gold Trust treats individual holders as owning their share of the underlying gold,
with long-term gains taxed as collectibles \citep{GLDTax2022,IRSTopic409}, and managed-futures fund
prospectuses report returns after taxes on distributions \citep{DBMFTax2026}. Rankings in taxable accounts
can therefore differ from those in tax-advantaged retirement accounts.

\section{Replication}
\label{app:reproducibility}

The \href{\gitrepo}{replication repository} contains the public source data, strategy definitions,
numerical results, and code to rebuild all exhibits. The
\gitlink{data/replication-panel-trend.csv}{annual return panel} and the
\gitlink{data/managed-futures-monthly.csv}{monthly managed-futures data} are the two data inputs. The
proprietary SG and BarclayHedge indexes are used only in Section~\ref{app:mf-external}, and their
redistribution is subject to the providers' terms.

The \gitlink{results/erc_refocusing/PROTOCOL.md}{experimental protocol} specifies the risk parity
estimation, the exposure grid, and the sensitivity analyses. The
\gitdirlink{results/erc_refocusing/n10000_final/}{result files} contain the risk parity weights, covariance
matrix, input checksums, and the complete results for each specification. The simulations use 10,000
paths, seed 20260827, and an average block length of ten years. Each alternative sample recomputes the
utility reference of the unlevered all-equity strategy. The
\gitlink{results/erc_refocusing/VALIDATION.md}{validation checks} confirm, among other identities, that
the path-matched income calculation reproduces the baseline ruin indicators exactly when each strategy is
assigned its own 4\% withdrawal. The bond-free strategies, the comparison at equal volatility, its sensitivity analyses
(\gitlink{build/eqvol_sensitivity.py}{eqvol\_sensitivity.py}), resampled histories
(\gitlink{build/historical_panel_bootstrap_eqvol.py}{historical\_panel\_bootstrap\_eqvol.py}) and
managed-futures variants (\gitlink{build/mf_variant_lifecycle.py}{mf\_variant\_lifecycle.py}), the Sharpe ratios in excess of bills, and
the comparison of the managed-futures series with commercial indexes by subperiod are produced by
\gitlink{build/revision_checks.py}{a separate script}, which reuses the same seed and reproduces the
baseline results exactly. The \gitlink{paper/new_paper/README.md}{build instructions} describe how
to regenerate the tables and figures from the result files.
